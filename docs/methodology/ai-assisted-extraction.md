# AI-Assisted Data Extraction — Methodology and Prompt Log

This document records how the working group used AI tools (Claude, Perplexity/OpenAI) to accelerate data extraction from the screened paper corpus during the systematic literature review, including the exact prompts used, the results of each iteration, and the corrections made between iterations. It is adapted from the group's internal "AI Logic-Prompts" working document.

> **Why this is here:** transparency about AI use in the review process is part of the group's methodology. Iteration 1 shows the first extraction attempt and its weaknesses; Iteration 2 shows how the prompts and inclusion/exclusion criteria were tightened in response.

## Iteration 1 — Spreadsheet creation and data extraction

**Prompt given to Claude:**

> "I want you to create a code for me that will connect you with this folder (Phase 2) and that will put in an Excel file the answers you gather from each PDF into a line of the Excel File. The questions are: "

### Extraction fields (Iteration 1)

**1. Study Identification & Context**
- Country/countries of study
- Venue (journal/conference) where published
- Institution type(s) (Engineering school, Liberal Arts, Public/Private, etc.)
- Program (CS, IT, CE, multiple, etc.)
- Number of institutions (single vs. multiple)

**2. Course/Intervention Context**
- Focus of paper (intervention study, experience report, other — specify)
- Course context (regular course, bootcamp, summer program, etc.)
- Course name/type (Intro to Programming, Software Engineering, Capstone, etc.)
- Level (intro, mid-program, capstone/final year)
- Elective vs. mandatory
- Intended student body (majors / non-majors / open to anyone)
- Teaching mode / course modality (online, traditional, flipped, etc.)
- Team mode (online, in-person, hybrid; synchronous/asynchronous)
- Intervention scope (single course, multiple courses, whole program)

**3. Sample & Participants**
- Classroom size
- Total N participating
- Sampling method (convenience, whole class, opt-in, incentivized — what incentive?)
- Specific population scope (e.g., women, minorities, other targeted group)
- Does the study control for demographic/confounding variables in design or analysis?

**4. Team Formation**
- How teams/groups were formed (instructor-assigned, self-selected, algorithmic, etc.)

**5. Conceptualizing Teamwork**
- How the paper defines teamwork/groupwork (with citation, if given)
- Is teamwork explicitly taught? If so, how and how much?
- Skills: which are named; scaffolding/integration approaches?
- Virtues: which are named; scaffolding/integration approaches?
- Values: which are named; scaffolding/integration approaches?
- How researchers/educators define "successful" teaming pedagogy (stated goals; how goal attainment is measured)
- How dysfunctional teams/members are identified and handled

**6. Tools, Platforms & External Stakeholders**
- Tools/platforms used to enact teamwork (CATME, Canvas, GitHub Classroom, Slack, Discord, etc.)
- External contributors/factors (industry mentor, client, another course/institution, open-source community, etc.)

**7. Assessment & Evaluation**
- How the course/intervention overall was assessed
- Weight of project/teamwork component in course grade
- How the team/project/deliverable itself was assessed
- If skills/virtues/values are mentioned, are they specifically evaluated, and how?
- Instruments used for assessment (named tools); results reported; deemed successful?
- What was assessed? (project, teamwork, product?)
- How was the outcome of teamwork evaluated? (presentation, report, code, etc.)

**8. Rigor & Limitations**
- Does the paper address its own limitations, and how?

### Iteration 1 outputs

- Extraction spreadsheet created by Claude (external link retained in the group's private Drive/Dropbox; not mirrored here).
- A comparable extraction script/code was independently generated by OpenAI/Perplexity for cross-checking.
- Both AI tools were also asked to export a transcript of their sessions for documentation; Claude reported that a verbatim PDF export wasn't possible on its side, while Perplexity was able to generate one.

### Iteration 1 review and lessons learned

Each author analyzed a 10% sample of the extraction results as a quality check. Findings:

- The AI extractor was a valuable accelerator ("mostly correct," "looks accurate" in many cases).
- It showed systematic error patterns on nuanced fields: relevance, modality, teamwork teaching/assessment, and distinguishing *research tools* from *teamwork tools*.
- The team identified a need to more tightly define "skills," "virtues," and "values," and to constrain the models to only the content of the papers themselves (no outside inference).

This is consistent with broader evidence that AI-assisted extraction works best as a "second reviewer" or triage tool, paired with explicit rules to mark items `NA` when uncertain and with structured human quality control for complex, conceptual variables — which motivated a second iteration.

## Iteration 2 — Refined prompts and inclusion/exclusion criteria

**Assumptions given to the models:**

- A final column must be populated based on all other fields and the paper's stated objective.
- "Future work" and "related work" sections are not treated as findings of the paper.
- No inferences beyond what is explicit in the paper (except very basic facts, e.g., a university's country).
- If a paper is under 4 pages or is a "Work in Progress," it is marked not accepted, with a comment noting it is a short/WIP paper.
- Be consistent: if information is not present, mark `NA`, never leave blank.

**Working definitions used for extraction:**

- **Skills** — the general skill level of an individual needed to participate effectively in teamwork, commonly including adaptability, coordination, decision-making, leadership, interpersonal development, and communication (De Prada et al., 2022).
- **Values** — from a neo-Aristotelian perspective, values are conceptualized as goods: what is of value is not defined solely by subjective interest, but can be valuable for humans in general, above and beyond an individual's preference (Fowers et al., 2021).
- **Virtues** — stable, well-motivated dispositions to act in self- and other-benefiting ways on the basis of knowledge about those actions (Fowers et al., 2021).

**Research questions driving extraction:**

- RQ1: How do computing and engineering faculty and researchers define teamwork?
- RQ2: How do computing and engineering faculty and researchers distinguish successful teamwork?
- RQ3: Which skills, values, and virtues (SVV) do computing and engineering faculty describe as aligning with or supporting teamwork in education?

**Refinements to the question template:**

- A downstream boolean question is only answered "Yes" if all prior boolean questions were "Yes."
- A paper is only considered if the majority of participants are undergraduates, or if it is a course offered to undergraduates.
- "Team mode" concerns how the team does its work (synchronous, asynchronous, in-person, online).
- "Course mode" concerns the instructional team's interaction with students (in-person, hybrid, online).
- New fields added at the end: whether the paper uses the word "virtue" in its body; the page and verbatim sentence if so; and a final yes/no relevance decision tied directly to RQ1–RQ3.

### Iteration 2 outcome (first pass)

Applying the refined prompts to both models before further tightening the inclusion/exclusion criteria produced the following accepted-paper counts (two independent runs shown per model/condition):

| Condition | Run 1 | Run 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Accepted using OpenAI model | 400 | 274 |
| Accepted using Claude model | 210 | 149 |
| Accepted by both models | 206 | 123 |
| Accepted by exactly one model | 198 | 177 |
| Not accepted by either model | 69 | 173 |

Extraction quality was good, but the models' final *inclusion/exclusion decisions* were not reliable enough, which prompted a further tightening of the decision criteria.

### Final inclusion / exclusion criteria

**Assumptions:**

- Analysis is restricted to the body of the paper (content prior to the references).
- "Related Work," "Related Studies," "Future Work," and "Future Studies" sections are excluded from consideration.
- No inferences beyond what is explicitly written by the authors.
- If information is not present, mark `NA`; never leave blank.

**Inclusion criteria:**

- The majority of participants in the course/intervention are undergraduates (more than 50%).
- The paper focuses on teamwork, teaming pedagogy, or team projects.

**Exclusion criteria:**

- Not a primary study (e.g., opinion pieces, surveys, or systematic literature reviews are excluded).
- The body of the paper is not in English.
- The body of the paper is less than 4 pages.
- The paper does not report on participants in higher/tertiary education.
- The paper reports on an intervention unrelated to a specific course (e.g., a university-wide hackathon).

**Expected output (per paper):**

- Paper file name
- Decision (accept/reject)
- If rejected, which exclusion criterion applied
